SPINE: Restoring Spatial Structure in Multimodal LLMs

MLBio, EPFL

Summary

Multimodal LLMs lose spatial structure long before they answer a spatial question. Using a spatial-structure metric, this project traces where the loss happens: CLIP’s deep layers trade spatial coherence for global semantics, the MLP projector induces a further 42% drop, and processing inside the LLM degrades what remains by 54% by mid-depth.

SPINE restores that structure in two steps. A training-free self-calibration step recovers spatial coherence from the frozen vision encoder’s intermediate layers (×2.9 on the structure metric); the calibrated features are then injected as residuals into early LLM layers and trained with a structure-preserving objective that counteracts degradation throughout the LLM.

Key findings

  • +3.9 average points across seven spatial-reasoning benchmarks, including +12.8 on What’sUp, while also improving grounding and general VQA.
  • A systematic study of multi-layer vision fusion (6 methods, 13 benchmarks) mapping the interplay between layer selection, injection depth, and alignment quality.
