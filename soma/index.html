SOMA: A Benchmark for Fine-Grained Egocentric Behavior and Body-Motion Understanding

Under review · Mathis Group for Computational Neuroscience & AI, EPFL
Matea Tashkovska, Naël Ouerghemi, Haozhe Qi, Alexander Mathis

Summary

Understanding human body motion matters for multimodal systems that observe and assist people, but evaluations of multimodal LLMs largely target objects, scenes, and coarse actions. Body motion is harder: it has to be inferred from the egocentric perspective, where the body itself is only partly visible and motion must be reconstructed from occluded and temporally distributed cues.

SOMA is a benchmark for fine-grained motion understanding from egocentric video, built on 1,632 human-verified questions across 12 tasks and three capability axes: body configuration, motion dynamics, and temporal reasoning.

Key findings

  • Across 37 MLLMs — closed-source, open-source, and specialized egocentric models — the strongest reaches 50.0% accuracy against 68.5% for human annotators.
  • Models are far better at recognizing what is happening than at reading how the body is configured or how motion unfolds over time.
  • Common failure modes point to missing capabilities rather than missing data, narrowing the gap to specific, addressable weaknesses.
