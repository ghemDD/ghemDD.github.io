EgoPraxis: Multimodal Modeling of Egocentric Human Motion

Master’s thesis · Mathis Group for Computational Neuroscience & AI, EPFL · In preparation
N. Ouerghemi, M. Tashkovska, A. Mathis

Summary

Egocentric video shows the world from the wearer’s viewpoint, but it rarely shows the wearer’s own body: apart from glimpses of the hands, posture, trunk, arms, and legs stay outside the field of view. Motion-capture suits and worn sensors record full-body motion synchronized with the video, yet vision-language models have no way to read it. This thesis asks whether a VLM that reads the wearer’s body motion reasons better about egocentric activity, and builds both the model and the benchmark needed to answer that question.

EgoPraxis treats full-body motion as a discrete token modality inside a pretrained vision-language backbone: a frozen product-quantized tokenizer turns continuous body motion into a code vocabulary the model reads and writes under the same next-token objective as text, and a three-stage curriculum trains a single model to reconstruct full-body motion from head- and wrist-mounted sensors, generate motion from language, caption and narrate body motion, and answer questions about the camera wearer’s actions.

EgoMotion-QA is the measuring instrument: a participant-disjoint benchmark of 35,710 multiple-choice questions over household (Nymeria) and kitchen (EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30) recordings, spanning eleven proprioceptive and temporal reasoning categories, with multi-solver blind filtering for quality control and a public evaluation protocol.

Key findings

  • EgoPraxis-4B reaches 60.0% on EgoMotion-QA, +14.1 points over the best zero-shot vision-language model in the 2B–12B range and +2.6 over a backbone-matched fine-tuned control, a margin that comes from the motion curriculum rather than motion input at test time.
  • The largest gains land on concurrent body state (+6.9) and before–after ordering (+6.5), the categories where video alone underdetermines the answer.
  • The same model reconstructs full-body motion from sparse worn sensors competitively with a reimplementation of EgoLM, narrates body motion, and generates it from text.
  • Probing shows the trained model reads its motion input rather than treating it as noise: denser frame sampling and larger video-only models do not close the reasoning gap.
