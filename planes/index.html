Planes, Not A380: How Prompting, Context, and Granularity Shape VLM Performance in Aerial Imagery

JSTARS · IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing
N. Ouerghemi, C. Tomoiaga, M. Detyniecki

Summary

A systematic study of how 9 VLMs—frontier MLLMs and contrastive vision encoders—handle referring object classification across 3 aerial datasets (iSAID, DIOR, FAIR1M) under open- and closed-world answer spaces. Built during a research internship at AXA.

Key findings

  • Models handle coarse categories well but collapse on fine-grained distinctions (e.g., specific aircraft types).
  • Coarse visual prompts that preserve scene context (oriented boxes, circles) outperform fine-grained prompts (contours, reverse masks), while neighboring-object context is a double-edged sword: boosting accuracy when relevant, hurting in cluttered scenes.
  • VLMs behave as multimodal in-context learners: same-category context examples drive fine-grained accuracy, with performance collapsing when they are removed (0.372 → 0.040 on FAIR1M).
