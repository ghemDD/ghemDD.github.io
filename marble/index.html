MARBLE: An Agentic Benchmark for Spatial Reasoning and Visual Abstraction

NeurIPS 2026 · Evaluations & Datasets track
Y. Jiang, N. Ouerghemi, Y. Chai, M. Brbić, M. Moor
Website arXiv

Summary

MARBLE is a diagnostic agentic benchmark for spatially grounded reasoning and planning across three gym-style environments—3D assembly, a dynamic maze, and pose-dependent block navigation—with difficulty controlled along two axes: observation format and planning horizon. Ten frontier MLLMs are evaluated against human baselines.

Key findings

  • Visual-to-spatial abstraction, not planning, is the dominant bottleneck: replacing human-annotated symbolic states with raw visual observations collapses success rates (e.g., 87.9% → 6.5% for Kimi-K2.6), while longer planning horizons mainly affect weaker models.
  • The strongest model reaches only 7.3% average success on the hard split.
  • Spatial state parsing probes isolate perception from planning: frontier MLLMs fail to extract structured spatial states that are trivial for humans, with an isometric perception gap (accuracy rises to 80–99% under flat 2D views) partially recovered by agentic code-execution parsing (up to +62 points).
