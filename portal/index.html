PORTAL: Rethinking Projector Training in Multimodal LLMs

NeurIPS 2026
F. Gröger*, N. Ouerghemi*, S. Wen*, M. Brbić  (*equal contribution)
Website arXiv Code

Summary

Projector alignment—the standard first stage of multimodal LLM training—turns out to be a coarse operation with a broad solution space: 10–20% of the standard 558k image-caption corpus recovers most downstream gains, and projectors trained on different datasets can be linearly interpolated while preserving performance.

PORTAL takes this observation to its limit: a training-free, closed-form projector built via optimal transport between the vision and LLM feature distributions—no paired data, no gradient steps—with a proof that second-order moment matching preserves pairwise cosine dispersion at the attention input.

Key findings

  • Matches the 558k-trained alignment pipeline across 6 LLM backbones, 2 vision encoders, and 16 benchmarks (within 1.6 average points, exceeding it in 3 of 7 settings).
  • As a warm start, it matches the trained baseline using 135× less training compute.
